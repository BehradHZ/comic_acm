THE THREE STONES
Complete Screenplay • Acts I–IV
WORKING MASTER DRAFT
This is the full-length story draft collected from our four acts, retaining the scene sequence and main
dialogue, with some stage directions consolidated for readability. The separate 15-page adaptation is
designed for comic production.

ACT I — THE FIRST BETRAYAL
Scene 1 — The Death of Creation
Darkness.
A faint yellow light illuminates a ruined stone chamber. Its enormous pillars have collapsed. Pieces of the
structure are scattered across the floor.
At the center of the destruction lies a LEGO figure dressed in yellow and gold.
Creation.
His yellow stone still glows faintly.
Another figure stands over him, clad in red.
Life.
The chamber is silent except for the sound of small pieces of debris falling from the ceiling.
        CREATION: ...So this is what you've decided.
Life doesn't answer.
        CREATION: After everything we built together?
        LIFE: You know why I'm doing this.
        CREATION: I know what you believe you're doing.
Life kneels beside him.
        LIFE: I asked you to help me. Both of you.
        CREATION: And we told you why we couldn't.
        LIFE: Then you left me no other way.
Creation looks at him for a long moment.
        CREATION: Did they ask you for this?
        LIFE: They beg for mercy every day.
        CREATION: That's not what I asked.
Silence. Life looks away.
        LIFE: I gave them life. I cannot keep watching them destroy it.
        CREATION: And you think this will save them?
        LIFE: ...I have to believe it will.
Creation's yellow stone begins to dim.
        CREATION: I wish you had believed in them instead.
The light around Creation fades. Life remains kneeling beside his oldest friend.
He reaches toward the yellow stone, but stops. For a moment, his hand hangs motionless. Then he takes it.
The yellow stone glows again beside his red one. Life stands.
In the distance, beyond the ruined chamber, a faint blue light shines. Life looks toward it.
Cut to black.


Scene 2 — Before There Was a World
An endless black void.


                                                                                     THE THREE STONES • Draft | 2
Caption: Before there was a world, there were three.
Three figures stand together, each holding a luminous stone. Blue. Yellow. Red.
There are no stars, no planets, no ground beneath them. Only the three gods.
    CREATION: An eternity of nothing.
    LIFE: You sound bored.
    CREATION: Wouldn't you be?
    WISDOM: What would you have us do?
Creation raises his yellow stone.
    CREATION: Make something.
He concentrates. A handful of tiny LEGO bricks materialize, slowly assembling into the outline of a figure. But
the figure cannot move. It has no awareness.
Creation examines it.
    CREATION: Well. It's a start.
Wisdom approaches, studying it with curiosity.
    WISDOM: It has form. But no understanding.
His blue stone glows. The figure's eyes illuminate for a moment, but it remains motionless.
    LIFE: And no life.
He steps forward, holding his red stone beside the others.
    LIFE: Perhaps none of us can do this alone.
The three exchange glances. One by one, they raise their stones.
The stones come together, forming the three-part symbol.
Three beams of light emerge, one from each stone. They spiral upward. They converge into a single brilliant
white beam. And then the light separates again, flowing back toward all three gods.
For the first time, all three share the power of the completed stones.
The void erupts into creation. Stars appear. Planets take shape. Oceans fill. Forests rise from LEGO bricks.
And in a small clearing, the first LEGO people come to life.
They look at their hands, their surroundings, and finally at each other. One reaches out to another. The other
takes their hand.
The three gods observe their creation.
    CREATION: They can build.
    WISDOM: They can learn.
    LIFE: And they can live.
The first LEGO people begin exploring their world.
Wisdom watches quietly. His gaze shifts briefly toward the now-separated stones. His expression is thoughtful.


Scene 3 — Wisdom's Secret
Time passes. The first settlements emerge. LEGO people construct homes, cultivate fields, and create tools.
The gods watch over their growing civilization.
One day, Wisdom descends to a small village. The people recognize him. They gather around him with
curiosity and admiration.


                                                                                   THE THREE STONES • Draft | 3
A young LEGO child approaches.
    CHILD: Did you really make the stars?
    WISDOM: Not by myself.
    CHILD: Can you make anything?
Wisdom considers the question.
    WISDOM: Not everything.
    CHILD: Then who tells you what to do?
Wisdom looks surprised.
    WISDOM: No one.
    CHILD: Oh.
The child thinks for a moment.
    CHILD: Then who tells us?
Wisdom looks around. People are kneeling before a statue depicting the three gods. Others have gathered
around a priest asking for their blessing.
He looks back at the child.
    WISDOM: That is something you'll have to discover for yourselves.
Later. Wisdom stands alone at the edge of the village. He looks at the people for a long time. Then at his blue
stone. He closes his hand around it.
A soft blue light spreads throughout the settlement. No destruction. No screams. The villagers continue their
lives. The statues remain. But something has changed.
The child from earlier notices Wisdom standing nearby.
    CHILD: Excuse me?
Wisdom turns.
    CHILD: Are you looking for someone?
Wisdom doesn't answer immediately.
    WISDOM: No.
He glances at the village.
    WISDOM: I think I've found what I was looking for.
The child smiles politely and walks away. Wisdom watches.
For the first time, the people no longer recognize their creator. He looks at his stone once more. His expression
is not one of triumph. He returns to the realm of the gods.
Caption: And in time, the gods became forgotten.


Scene 4 — Eons
Caption: Centuries passed. Then millennia.
A sequence of images.
Villages become cities. Cities become kingdoms. LEGO people discover new technologies. They build bridges,
monuments, ships, and machines. They discover new lands. Their civilizations flourish.
And then...
A king looks over a map. He places a red marker on a neighboring kingdom. Soldiers march. A banner rises.
The same banner is planted in the ruins of a conquered city.

                                                                                    THE THREE STONES • Draft | 4
Buildings burn. A soldier pulls another from their home. A field of crops is set ablaze to deny food to an
approaching army. Families flee.
A small LEGO child sits alone beside the remains of a destroyed house.
The image changes. Different flags. Different kingdoms. Different centuries. The same violence.
An army marches over the ruins of a civilization that had once conquered another. A wealthy ruler celebrates
while people beyond his palace walls go hungry. Two soldiers fight over a piece of land neither will live to see
prosper.
The years keep passing. And the wars keep coming.
High above the world, Life watches. His red stone pulses faintly.
He sees thousands of lives begin. He sees thousands end. Again. And again. And again.
He looks away. But he can still feel them. Every life lost. Every life he once helped create.
Back on the ground. A vast golden wheat field stretches toward the horizon. Or what remains of it.
The land has been scarred by war. Parts of the field have burned, and the irrigation canals have collapsed.
Three young farmers work beside a broken channel. They are friends, but visibly exhausted.
One studies a rough diagram scratched into a wooden board.
    FARMER 1: If we divert the water from the western canal, we can save the lower fields.
    FARMER 2: And leave the eastern farms dry?
    FARMER 1: Then what do you suggest?
The second farmer looks at the damaged irrigation network.
    FARMER 2: There has to be a way to share it.
The third farmer quietly examines the diagram. The other two begin arguing over the available water.
The third picks up a piece of charcoal and starts drawing another route. We don't see the completed drawing.
The scene cuts away.
Above the world, Life stands motionless. His stone glows as another distant settlement burns.
    LIFE: Enough.


Scene 5 — The Disagreement
The three gods stand together in their ancient chamber. The same place where they have met for countless
ages. But this time, Life has summoned the others.
    WISDOM: You asked us to come.
    LIFE: I've watched them.
    CREATION: We all have.
    LIFE: No. You've watched what they build. What they discover. How far they've come.
He steps toward the center of the chamber.
    LIFE: I've watched how many of them never get that chance.
He raises his red stone. Images of wars and suffering appear around the chamber.
    LIFE: They murder each other for land. For wealth. For names and banners that will be forgotten in a
    century.
An image shows the burning fields.
    LIFE: They destroy the very things that keep them alive.



                                                                                      THE THREE STONES • Draft | 5
Another image: a child standing in the ruins of a village.
    LIFE: And the innocent suffer alongside the guilty.
The images vanish.
    LIFE: We gave them everything they needed to live.
    WISDOM: We gave them the ability to decide how.
    LIFE: And look what they've decided!
Silence. Life lowers his voice.
    LIFE: I've thought about this for a long time.
    CREATION: About what?
    LIFE: We made a mistake.
The other two gods exchange glances.
    LIFE: When we created them, we gave them the freedom to do whatever they wished.
    WISDOM: Yes.
    LIFE: And that included destroying one another.
He looks directly at Wisdom.
    LIFE: What if it didn't?
    CREATION: What are you suggesting?
    LIFE: They could still think. Learn. Build. Love. They could have everything they have now.
He pauses.
    LIFE: Except the freedom to harm one another.
Wisdom is silent.
    CREATION: You want to take away their choices?
    LIFE: Not all of them.
    WISDOM: Only the ones you disapprove of.
    LIFE: The ones that cause suffering!
    WISDOM: And who decides which choices those are?
    LIFE: We do.
Wisdom looks at him.
    WISDOM: For how long?
    LIFE: For as long as it takes.
    WISDOM: And when they disagree with us?
    LIFE: They won't need to.
    WISDOM: Then they won't be free.
    LIFE: They'll be alive.
Life looks at Creation.
    LIFE: Tell me you understand.
Creation looks down at his yellow stone.
    CREATION: I understand why you want this.
Life looks relieved for a brief moment.


                                                                               THE THREE STONES • Draft | 6
    CREATION: But I won't help you do it.
    LIFE: Why?
    CREATION: Because we didn't create them just to follow our instructions.
    LIFE: We created them to live!
    CREATION: And living means making mistakes. Sometimes terrible ones.
    LIFE: Mistakes?
Life looks incredulous.
    LIFE: Entire civilizations have been wiped out. Children have died in wars they never chose to fight.
His voice breaks slightly.
    LIFE: I gave them life. I watched them take it from one another for ages.
He looks between the other two gods.
    LIFE: And you ask me to keep watching?
Neither answers immediately.
    WISDOM: No.
Life turns to him.
    WISDOM: I'm asking you to understand what you're proposing.
    LIFE: I understand perfectly.
    WISDOM: You want to end cruelty by making cruelty impossible.
    LIFE: Yes.
    WISDOM: And kindness?
    LIFE: What about it?
    WISDOM: If they cannot choose otherwise, what does kindness mean?
Life considers this.
    LIFE: Does it matter, if no one has to suffer?
    WISDOM: I believe it does.
    LIFE: Then you would let them suffer for the sake of a principle.
    WISDOM: And you would make their choices for them for the sake of your certainty.
Life's red stone glows brighter.
    LIFE: You call it certainty. I call it responsibility.
    CREATION: We are responsible for creating them.
    LIFE: Then act like it!
The chamber trembles slightly. Life realizes his anger is affecting the stone. Its glow subsides.
For a moment, all three stand in silence.
    LIFE: We have the power to end this.
    WISDOM: Not without taking away something that makes them who they are.
    LIFE: And you both refuse?
    CREATION: We do.
Life turns toward Wisdom.
    LIFE: Even you?


                                                                                     THE THREE STONES • Draft | 7
Wisdom holds his gaze.
    WISDOM: Especially me.
Life looks at his two oldest companions. The only beings who have ever known him as an equal.
    LIFE: I thought, of everyone in existence, you two would understand.
He turns to leave.
    CREATION: Life.
Life stops.
    CREATION: We can still find another way.
Life doesn't turn around.
    LIFE: How many more have to die while we look for one?
No answer. Life walks away. The red light disappears into the darkness.
Creation looks at Wisdom.
    CREATION: Do you think he'll come around?
Wisdom stares at the place where Life disappeared.
    WISDOM: I don't know.
For a moment, his eyes move toward the blue stone in his hand. Then back toward the darkness.
Silence.
End of Act I.

ACT II — THE LAST OF THE GODS
Scene 6 — The Last Conversation
The realm of the gods. An enormous chamber stands suspended above the LEGO world. Beyond its open
arches, stars stretch into the darkness.
At its center sits Wisdom. Before him rests a small, unfinished LEGO figure. One of the first forms Creation
ever made. Wisdom gently turns it over in his hands.
A distant rumble echoes through the chamber. Wisdom looks up. Red light spills across the floor.
Life enters. In one hand, he holds his own red stone. In the other, Creation's yellow stone.
Wisdom looks at the yellow stone. He understands.
    WISDOM: Where is he?
Life doesn't answer.
    WISDOM: Life.
    LIFE: He's gone.
Wisdom looks down at the unfinished LEGO figure. He sets it carefully on the table. A long silence.
    WISDOM: Did he fight you?
    LIFE: He tried to stop me.
    WISDOM: Of course he did.
Life takes a step forward.
    LIFE: It didn't have to end that way.
Wisdom looks at him.
    WISDOM: No. It didn't.
Life looks away.
    LIFE: You know why I'm here.
    WISDOM: I do.
    LIFE: Then you know what I'm asking.
    WISDOM: You're not asking anymore.
Silence.
    LIFE: I need your stone. And I need to know how the three are used together.
    WISDOM: And if I refuse?
Life looks at the yellow stone.
    LIFE: Please don't.
Wisdom rises slowly.
    WISDOM: You've already killed one of us.
    LIFE: To save countless others.
    WISDOM: And how many more would you sacrifice to save them?
    LIFE: As many as I must.
Wisdom's expression changes. Not anger. Sadness.
    WISDOM: That answer frightens me more than anything you've done.


                                                                                     THE THREE STONES • Draft | 9
    LIFE: You think I enjoy this?
    WISDOM: No.
Wisdom steps closer.
    WISDOM: That's what frightens me.
Life says nothing.


Scene 7 — The Weight of a Choice
Wisdom walks toward an enormous opening overlooking the LEGO world. Far below, tiny lights mark the
cities and villages of legokind.
    WISDOM: Do you remember when we first watched them build?
    LIFE: What?
    WISDOM: Their first shelter. Creation was so proud of them.
A faint smile crosses Wisdom's face.
    WISDOM: He'd spent so long making things for them. And there they were, making something for
    themselves.
Life approaches.
    LIFE: And now they build machines to destroy one another.
    WISDOM: Some do.
    LIFE: Entire kingdoms, Wisdom. Entire generations.
    WISDOM: I know.
    LIFE: No. You study their suffering. I feel it.
Life raises his red stone. Thousands of tiny red lights appear across the view of the world below. Each
represents a living being. Some lights flicker and disappear.
    LIFE: Every life that begins, I feel it.
More lights appear.
    LIFE: Every life that ends...
Several lights vanish.
    LIFE: ...I feel that too.
He closes his hand. The lights disappear.
    LIFE: I can't stop hearing them.
Wisdom watches his friend.
    WISDOM: I'm sorry.
    LIFE: Don't be sorry. Help me.
    WISDOM: I cannot give you what you want.
    LIFE: You mean you won't.
    WISDOM: Yes.
Life shakes his head.
    LIFE: I don't understand you.
    WISDOM: I know.

    LIFE: You know everything about how their minds work. You understand their decisions, their desires,
    their mistakes.
He gestures toward the world.
    LIFE: Surely you can see what needs to be done.
    WISDOM: I can see what you intend to do.
    LIFE: Then tell me where I'm wrong.
    WISDOM: You believe that because you gave them life, you know what their lives should be.
    LIFE: I know they shouldn't be killing each other!
    WISDOM: On that, we agree.
    LIFE: Then why are we fighting?
    WISDOM: Because you aren't offering them peace.
Wisdom turns to face him.
    WISDOM: You're offering them obedience.
Life's grip tightens around his stones.
    LIFE: Obedience is a small price to pay for a world without suffering.
    WISDOM: You don't get to decide that price for everyone.
    LIFE: Somebody has to!
The chamber trembles. Life realizes his voice has risen. He looks away, struggling to regain his composure.
Wisdom waits.
    LIFE: I thought you, of all beings, would see reason.
    WISDOM: And I thought you would remember why we gave them the freedom to choose.
    LIFE: We made a mistake!
    WISDOM: Perhaps.

    LIFE: What?
    WISDOM: Perhaps we did make mistakes. Perhaps some of our decisions brought suffering we never

Wisdom looks down at his blue stone.
    WISDOM: I've made decisions I still question.

    WISDOM: But if we take away their ability to choose, we take away their ability to become anything

    LIFE: They could become better.
    WISDOM: They could become exactly what you want.
Silence.
    LIFE: And what would you have me do? Nothing?
    WISDOM: You've spent so long looking at how their lives end.
Wisdom turns back toward him.
    WISDOM: When did you last look at how they live?

Wisdom doesn't respond.
    LIFE: I have watched enough. I've thought about this for centuries.
He raises the yellow stone.
    LIFE: And now I can finally do something about it.

    WISDOM: Then I suppose there's nothing left I can say.

Scene 8 — The Battle of Two Against One
The chamber falls silent. The three stones glow. Red. Yellow. Blue.
Life is the first to move. He thrusts the yellow stone forward. A wall of LEGO bricks erupts from the floor,
rushing toward Wisdom.
Wisdom doesn't move immediately. His blue stone glows. For a fraction of a second, we see several translucent
blue outlines of the possible ways the wall might strike.
Wisdom steps to the left. The wall passes inches from his face.
Life creates another structure, a massive pillar descending from above. Wisdom moves before the pillar even
begins to fall. It crashes into the floor where he stood.
    LIFE: You can see my attacks.
    WISDOM: I can see where they might lead.
Life creates a series of sharp stone formations rising rapidly from the floor. Wisdom moves between them with
almost unnatural precision. He isn't teleporting. He's moving exactly where he needs to be before the attacks
arrive.
Life changes tactics. Rather than attacking Wisdom directly, he raises enormous walls, cutting off different
paths through the chamber.
Wisdom's blue stone pulses. The reader sees a branching web of possible movements. Some lead to dead ends.
Others to the edge of the chamber. Wisdom selects a path and runs.
Life tilts a huge section of the floor upward. Wisdom jumps onto a collapsing platform, slides beneath an
emerging wall, and lands behind Life.
Before Life can turn, Wisdom strikes. Life falls against a pillar. The yellow stone slips from his hand. Wisdom
catches it.
For a moment, Wisdom holds the blue and yellow stones. Life looks up. The balance has changed.
    WISDOM: It's over.
    LIFE: No.
Wisdom raises the yellow stone. The walls Life created begin closing inward, trapping him.
    WISDOM: Please. Let it end here.
    LIFE: You'd take away my chance to save them?
    WISDOM: I'd stop you from taking away theirs.


                                                                                   THE THREE STONES • Draft | 12
Wisdom reaches toward Life's red stone. Life looks down. For a moment he seems defeated.
Then his red stone pulses. Tiny roots emerge through the cracks in the floor. Wisdom notices. His blue stone
flashes. He sees what Life is about to attempt.
The roots multiply, splitting into dozens of paths. They race through cracks and joints of LEGO bricks. Life's
power is not shaping the stone; it's accelerating the life already growing beneath it.
Roots force apart ancient masonry. One wall cracks. Then another. The chamber shakes.
Wisdom looks at the ceiling. His stone shows possible collapses: dozens, hundreds. Each new movement
changes the others.
Wisdom uses Creation's stone to reinforce the floor beneath him. Life breaks free from the damaged wall and
charges. Wisdom anticipates the attack and shifts aside.
Life misses. Wisdom creates a barrier. Life strikes it, dislodging bricks. Wisdom reinforces it.
Life steps back, breathing heavily. His arm is damaged from the collapse. The red stone glows; the injury
begins to heal.
Wisdom studies him. For the first time, there's uncertainty on his face. He's seen countless possible outcomes,
but Life will endure injuries no ordinary opponent could survive, and the red stone lets him recover. Wisdom
cannot.


The Turning Point
    LIFE: You always did think faster than the rest of us.
Wisdom doesn't respond.
    LIFE: But you still have to choose where to stand.
Life places his hand on the ground. His red stone glows. Roots and vines spread through the damaged
chamber again—not toward Wisdom, but toward the remaining supports.
Wisdom's blue stone lights up. He sees ceiling collapses, splitting floors, the entire chamber falling apart. He
can avoid some of it, but not all.
Wisdom raises Creation's stone and reinforces the supports one after another. Life keeps advancing. Wisdom
repairs one structure; two more begin to fail. More cracks appear.
Life forces Wisdom to spend Creation's power preserving the chamber instead of defending himself.
    WISDOM: You'll bring the whole place down.
    LIFE: Then stop holding it up.
Wisdom understands. His eyes go to the table, where Creation's unfinished LEGO figure still rests. The
chamber is the last place the gods ever shared as equals.
Wisdom releases the supports. He turns the yellow stone toward Life. A massive cage of interlocking bricks
assembles around him.
Life is trapped; the roots stop spreading. Wisdom takes a step forward, exhausted. The ceiling continues to
crumble, but the cage holds.
    LIFE: You can't keep me here forever.
    WISDOM: I don't need forever.
Wisdom approaches. His blue stone reveals a narrow opening in the cage through which he can retrieve the
red stone.
He reaches through. Life suddenly releases his grip on the red stone. It falls. Wisdom instinctively follows it
with his eyes.

Life grabs Wisdom's wrist. The blue stone flashes; Wisdom has already seen this possibility. He pulls back, but
not quickly enough.
Life twists Wisdom's wrist against the cage. The yellow stone falls from Wisdom's grip. Life catches it.
The cage collapses. Wisdom jumps backward. Life raises the yellow stone.
For one brief moment, Wisdom sees the paths ahead. There is no safe route.
A massive section of the chamber crashes down. Dust and bricks fill the air.
Silence.


Scene 9 — The Last God Standing
The dust settles. Most of the chamber is gone. Where the three gods once stood together, only broken pillars
remain.
Life emerges from beneath the rubble, injured. His red stone pulses, slowly repairing the damage.
He notices a faint blue glow beneath a collapsed section of the chamber.
Wisdom lies among the debris, his blue stone beside him. Its light is weak.
Life stops. For a moment, he looks almost relieved to see his friend.
    LIFE: Wisdom.
Wisdom opens his eyes and tries to move.
    LIFE: Don't.
Life kneels, looks at his red stone. Wisdom notices.
    WISDOM: Don't waste your strength.
Life hesitates, then lowers the stone.
    LIFE: I didn't want this.
    WISDOM: I know.
A long silence. Life looks toward the ruined chamber.
    LIFE: We could have done this together.
    WISDOM: We did.
Life looks at him.
    WISDOM: Once.
Wisdom's gaze drifts to the unfinished LEGO figure among the rubble. Life picks it up. One arm has come
loose; he gently presses it into place and sets it beside Wisdom.
    WISDOM: He never finished that one.
    LIFE: No.
    WISDOM: He said he wanted to see what they'd make of themselves first.
Life looks down at the figure.
Wisdom's blue stone grows dimmer.
    LIFE: When this is done...
    WISDOM: What?
    LIFE: When I've made things right... I hope you'll understand.
Wisdom watches him for a long moment.
    WISDOM: I do understand you.

Life looks almost relieved.
    WISDOM: I just wish you understood them.
Wisdom closes his eyes. The blue light fades.

Red. Yellow. Blue. All three stones.
For the first time since the universe's creation, one being possesses them all.
Life looks toward the world below. He has achieved what he set out to do. There is no triumph on his face.

Doubt.

ACT III — THE INHERITANCE
Scene 10 — The Weight of Wisdom
Silence. The once magnificent chamber of the gods lies in ruins. Life stands alone, holding the three stones: red,
yellow, blue.
For countless ages, they belonged to three beings who called one another friends. Now, two are gone.
Life looks toward Wisdom's body. The unfinished LEGO figure still rests beside him. Then he turns to the world
below: cities, villages, forests, oceans, and countless lives, unaware of what has happened above them.
    LIFE: It's over.
He looks at the stones.
    LIFE: No more wars. No more suffering.
He raises his hand to bring them together. But he stops. The blue stone catches his eye. Its light reflects across
his face.
    WISDOM (memory): I do understand you.
    WISDOM (memory): I just wish you understood them.
Life lowers his hand. For a moment, he looks almost angry.
    LIFE: I do understand them. I've watched them since the beginning.
Silence. He looks at the blue stone again.
    LIFE: ...Haven't I?
He holds it closer. Its light grows brighter. For the first time, Life consciously uses the power of Wisdom.


Seeing Beyond
The world below draws closer. Life's vision settles upon a battlefield: burning buildings, fallen soldiers, fleeing
families. All familiar horrors.
    LIFE: There. Again.
He watches a soldier strike down an enemy. But the blue stone continues to glow. The scene doesn't disappear.
Instead, Life notices something he had never seen before.
The soldier drops his weapon, kneels beside the fallen enemy, pulls a bandage from his pack, and tends the
wounds of the person he'd just been fighting.
Life watches, confused.
The scene shifts to a devastated village. The same child Life once saw sitting alone beside a ruined home. But
now he sees what happened afterward.
An elderly LEGO woman approaches, kneels, and offers her hand. The child eventually takes it. She leads the
child to survivors rebuilding a shelter. Days pass. The child helps carry bricks. Years pass. The child, now
grown, rebuilds the village. It prospers again.
Life watches in silence.
The scene shifts to an ordinary city. A LEGO person carrying a bag of food stops beside a stray cat. The cat
cautiously approaches. The person kneels and offers it food. The cat eats; the person smiles.
Life stares. No great invention, no conquest, no extraordinary achievement. Just one living being choosing to
care for another.




                                                                                    THE THREE STONES • Draft | 16
More scenes appear around him: people planting trees in a burned forest; a teacher helping a struggling
student; a stranger sharing food; a doctor working through the night; two people reconciling; an inventor
trying again after a failed machine; a family celebrating a birth; people embracing, mourning, laughing,
making mistakes, learning from them.
Not all of them are happy, but all of them are real.
Life steps back. He begins to recognize patterns he never considered. The wars were real. The suffering wasn't
exaggerated. But neither were these moments. All came from the same freedom he'd sworn to destroy.
He closes his hand. The visions disappear.
    LIFE: Wisdom...
His voice trails off. He looks at the stones, then the world. His expression hardens again.
    LIFE: No. It doesn't change what they've done. What they keep doing.
He looks to the distant cities.
